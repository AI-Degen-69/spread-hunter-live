# Trial run: shadow-06, depth/volume gate trial for #88, 2026-09-01

The controlled experiment issue #88 asked for. Everything below is measured from
`data/06_shadow_01-09_trial88.db`, `runtime/near_misses.jsonl` and the registry
the run wrote — no estimates, no projections. Where a figure could not be
measured it says so.

Read [the baseline](2026-08-31-shadow-05-baseline.md) first. A trial is only
interpretable against a baseline taken under the same conditions, and that
document is the reference this one is measured against.

**Result in one line: the hypothesis behind #88 is not supported. Halving the
depth and volume bars did not produce shallower queues — the queues measured
roughly twice as deep — and the trial also showed why the lever was weaker than
it looked.**

## Why the lever was weaker than it looked

The trial moved the depth bar from $500 to $250. Measured across 445
depth-rejected observations in `runtime/near_misses.jsonl`:

| Depth of the rejected market | Count | Share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| below $250 — the trial bar did **not** rescue it | 404 | 90.8% |
| $250 to $500 — rescued **only** by the trial | 41 | 9.2% |
| $500 or more | 0 | 0% |

Median depth of a depth-rejected market: **$47.32**. Maximum: $488.08.

The rejected population is not clustered just under the shipped bar. It sits an
order of magnitude below it. Halving the bar moved the line through mostly empty
space, and the 9.2% it did rescue are markets with $250–$500 of top-of-book
depth, which is thin by any reading.

The volume bar was weaker still. Every market the trial actually adopted carried
24-hour volume far above even the shipped $125,000 bar — $683,922, $386,121,
$232,233, $214,473, $205,789 — with one exception at $116,547. The volume
relaxation admitted essentially nothing that the shipped bar would have excluded.

## The measurement that reframes the problem

While computing the above, one figure turned out to explain the fill rate better
than any gate does. Sum of the two legs' mid prices, from paired quote
observations in both stores:

| | Baseline shadow-05 | Trial shadow-06 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paired observations | 108 | 120 |
| `mid_UP + mid_DOWN` median | **1.0000** | **1.0000** |
| minimum | 0.9800 | 0.9950 |
| maximum | 1.0050 | 1.0050 |
| share below 1.00 | 6.5% | 3.3% |
| Our maker pair cost (median) | 0.9500 | 0.9400 |
| Edge demanded (1.00 − pair cost) | **0.0500** | **0.0600** |

The paired mid sum is $1.0000 at the median, with observed values ranging from
$0.9800 to $1.0050. We rest orders that ask to buy the pair for $0.94–$0.95,
which means asking to be filled roughly 3 cents below mid on each leg. That is
the quoting configuration working as designed —
`reward_offset = 0.020` plus `price_risk_widen = 0.010`
(`core_brain/config.py:76`, `core_brain/config.py:743`) — and it is a plausible
explanation for both the 0.5% fill rate and the thousands of shares standing
ahead of us: a fill requires someone to cross several cents through mid.

The same arithmetic rules out the obvious alternative without needing a run.
Crossing the spread as a taker means paying the ask on both legs, which is
mid plus roughly half the spread on each. At the median $1.0000 paired mid sum,
a taker pair costs at least $1.00 before the spread is counted. Taking is not a
route to the same trade at a worse price; it is not a route to the trade at all.

## Conclusions

1. **#88 is answered, negatively.** Halving the depth and volume bars did not
   reduce queue depth. Every cancel class measured deeper, and the overall
   median roughly doubled. There is no evidence here for adopting the lower
   bars.
2. **The lever was smaller than the issue assumed.** 90.8% of depth-rejected
   markets sit below even the trial bar, at a median of $47. There was no
   near-miss population waiting just under $500 to be harvested.
3. **The volume bar was not binding for most observed adopted markets.** Five
   cleared the shipped $125,000 bar, but one observed adopted market was below
   that baseline at $116,547.
4. **The shipped bars are, if anything, vindicated.** The baseline's own
   conclusion — that gates account for 56% of cancels and are doing their job —
   survives this run: 62% of trial cancels were gates, and the run's two fills
   were both single legs that the safety path exited within seconds.
5. **The next lever is how far from mid we stand, not which queues we stand in.**
   The baseline's conclusion 5 pointed at the gates. This run measured the gates
   and found them innocent, and measured a 5–6 cent edge demand against a book
   that prices the pair at exactly fair. That is the untested parameter.

## What this trial does NOT establish

- **Nothing causal about the bars.** The trial ran at 23:33–00:48 against the
  baseline's 20:43–21:58, on a partly different set of markets. Lower bars and
  a later hour are confounded, and this run cannot separate them. The direction
  is strong enough not to support #88; it is not strong enough to prove that
  lower bars *cause* deeper queues.
- **Nothing about profitability.** Zero merged pairs. The −$0.35 is two
  single-leg safety exits, not a strategy result.
- **Nothing about fill rates.** 2 fills against 2 fills is not a comparison. Any
  reading of a trend in single-digit fill counts is noise.
- **Nothing about adverse selection.** `markouts` is empty in both stores — no
  horizon matured in either run. If a future change does start producing fills,
  there is currently no measurement telling us whether those fills are good
  ones. That gap blocks the interpretation of any follow-up experiment.
- **Nothing about the edge-demand finding beyond correlation.** The mid-sum
  measurement is consistent across both runs and explains the fill rate well,
  but no run has yet varied `reward_offset` to test it.

## Reproducing the figures

```powershell
python -m core_brain.cancel_report --db data/06_shadow_01-09_trial88.db
python -m core_brain.cancel_report --db data/04_shadow_31-08_20-43.db
```

Queue percentiles come from `orders.cancel_queue_ahead` grouped by
`orders.cancel_reason`. The mid-sum table pairs `quotes.mid` for the `UP` and
`DOWN` sides of the same `condition_id` within the same second, and compares
their sum to the sum of `quotes.price` for the same pair. The near-miss depth
distribution is read from the `greens[].depth_measured` field of
`runtime/near_misses.jsonl`, restricted to records whose `cause` names depth.
